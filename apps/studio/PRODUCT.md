# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Creators who make short and long videos for YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and LinkedIn: solo creators, small brand and marketing teams, and educators who explain things. They want videos that look edited by a pro and hold up to scrutiny, without spending a day in a timeline. They work in the browser, on a laptop most of the time and on a phone for quick edits.

## Product Purpose

Crewcut is a video editor where a crew of AI agents does the heavy lifting (finding ideas, researching, scripting, fact-checking, animating, voicing, publishing, reading results) and a person approves every key step or takes over the edit at any time. It also works as a hands-on editor with templates, transitions, keyframes and captions. Success is a creator going from an idea to a published, sourced video they're proud of, in one sitting.

## Positioning

The video editor for you and your AI crew: agents make the video, you make the calls, and every fact in it links to its source.

## Conversion & proof

The marketing landing page (`/`, signed-out visitors) uses the brand register; the app itself is product.

- Primary CTA: "Start free". Secondary: "See it work" (scrolls to the live demo).
- The line a visitor remembers: "The video editor for you and your AI crew."
- Belief ladder: this is a real editor, not a toy generator → the agents do real work I'd otherwise do myself → I stay in control (I approve each step, I can edit anything) → I can trust what it says (every claim has a source) → it's free to try now.
- Proof on hand: none from customers yet. The product is the proof: the Proof report, sourced claims, the live editor and templates. Credit the tools honestly (built with Agent-Native, Remotion, ElevenLabs, Pexels). No invented logos, user counts or testimonials.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, honest, playful. Sharp like a clean jump cut: confident craft, precise type, nothing wasted. Honest like a receipt: plain words, real product, claims backed by sources. Playful like the logo's slipped half: a wink in the motion, never silly. Talk like a creator, not a platform; standard editing words are fine, internal or AI jargon is not.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI SaaS: purple gradients, glowing orbs, sparkle icons everywhere, "supercharge your workflow" copy.
- Cream editorial magazine: beige page, italic serif headlines, thin rules, no product shown.
- Template-y CapCut clone: busy feature grids, stock photos of people laughing at laptops.
- Dev-tool terminal: monospace everything, black-and-green, code snippets as decoration.

## Design Principles

- Show the real thing. The product is the hero: live editor, real templates, real reports, never mock-ups of features that don't exist.
- Receipts over claims. Every promise on the page is something a visitor can see working or check.
- People decide. The agents are a crew, the person is the director; copy and visuals keep the human in the frame.
- Motion like an edit. Cuts, wipes and slips borrowed from video editing, used with timing and restraint.
- Plain words. If a creator wouldn't say it, the page doesn't either.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast, full keyboard use, visible focus, meaningful alt text and captions on demo media. Every animation has a reduced-motion version that shows the same content without movement.
